# Clave Currents — an algorithmic philosophy

**Clave Currents** treats motion as partnership. Nothing in the system moves alone: every visible form is a
*pair* of silk strands bound to a shared centre of gravity, the way two dancers share an axis. The centre
drifts through a slow, layered gradient-noise field — the room, the floor, the night — while the two strands
orbit it, separating and rejoining. The work is never a picture of dancers; it is the invisible geometry that
remains when two bodies have moved through space together, rendered as ribbons of light.

Time is not continuous in this world; it is *counted*. A hidden rhythmic lattice — a two-bar cycle with five
accents placed at 0, 1½, 3, 5 and 6 beats — governs the orbit. Between accents the partners rotate gently;
on each accent the orbital angle receives an eased half-turn, so the strands spin around each other exactly
where the pattern speaks. Only someone who knows the music will notice why the turns feel inevitable. The
orbital radius breathes on a slower cycle, alternating between close embrace and open position, so the ribbons
braid tightly, then bloom apart.

Every ribbon is computed analytically as a function of time rather than accumulated frame by frame. Its trail
is the history of the pair, re-evaluated at each instant by sampling the past along the same equations, which
makes the system perfectly seekable: any frame can be produced in isolation and the film can be rendered in
parallel without drift. This determinism is part of the craft — a meticulously crafted algorithm whose every
state is reproducible from a single seed, the product of deep computational expertise rather than luck.

Silk is suggested, not drawn. Each strand's width tapers from head to tail and is multiplied by the cosine of
a slowly accumulating twist, so the ribbon appears to flip and show its darker underside. Colour follows a
warm nocturnal palette — burgundy, crimson, rose and gold — mapped along the strand and lifted with additive
light where strands overlap, so crossings glow like stage light caught in fabric. A sparse field of gold dust
drifts on the same noise field, tying the ribbons to the air they cut through.

The parameters are few and each one was tuned with painstaking care: pair count, flow scale, orbital radius,
trail length, twist rate and glow. The balance is deliberate — enough density to feel lush, enough darkness
to keep typography legible on top. A master-level implementation should feel effortless, as a good lead feels
effortless: the structure is rigorous, but what the eye receives is only grace.
